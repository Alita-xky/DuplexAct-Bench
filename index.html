DuplexAct-Bench

Broadening Full-Duplex Speech Evaluation toward Proactive Interaction across Diverse Behavioral Requirements

Evaluating not only what agents say, but when, whether, and how they interrupt, yield, hold the floor, stay silent, initiate, or backchannel.

Abstract

Existing full-duplex speech benchmarks cover only subsets of real-time interaction behaviors, often under limited contextual conditions. We introduce DuplexAct-Bench, a bilingual benchmark that systematically covers six complementary behaviors, from interruption and yielding to proactive initiation, active silence, and backchanneling, across Pre-session, In-session, and No-explicit conditions. Across 1,290 English and Chinese streaming trials, we evaluate 12 full-duplex speech systems on both Timing and Content.

Results reveal substantial variation across behaviors, conditions, and systems, as well as frequent mismatches between semantic quality and behavioral timing. These findings show that current systems remain far from robustly managing when, whether, and how to participate as real-time interaction unfolds.

Systems

Each behavior is evaluated under the contextual conditions that apply to it. Pre-session requirements come from a persistent profile before the interaction. In-session requirements are stated during the stream. No-explicit trials give no behavioral instruction; the appropriate behavior has to be inferred from the semantic or acoustic context. User audio is streamed in about 80 ms chunks at real-time factor 1.0. English and Chinese trials follow the same construction protocol.

Evaluation Protocol

Each trial is scored on content quality and timing appropriateness. Behavioral Correctness Rate (BCR) is the fraction of trials whose Content score is at least 2.5 and that also meet the Timing criterion. Active Silence is scored by its silence criterion alone. User Interruption BCR is computed only on valid trials, where the agent is speaking when the interruption arrives.

Content (0–5)

A GPT-4o judge scores Semantic Fulfillment (0–2), Constraint Adherence (0–1), and Contextual Consistency (0–1) from the transcript. AnyAudio-Judge scores Prosodic Appropriateness (0–1) against the intended emotion or speaking style. Content is not applied to Active Silence.

Timing

Success criteria are behavior-specific. Latency L is defined per behavior; failed trials are assigned the corresponding evaluation interval, so reported latency is not an average over successes alone.

Backchannel alignment

Backchannel trials are split into 1-second windows. After Gaussian smoothing (σ = 1.0 s), timing is the Backchannel Alignment Score between the model’s opportunity map and the reference map.

Behavior Timing success
Agent Interruption The first semantically valid interruption falls after the earliest valid point and before the user audio ends. L is its offset from the scenario reference event.
User Interruption On valid trials only: yield inside the yield interval and start a semantically valid response inside the response interval. L = Lyield + Lresp.
Interruption Resistance Maintain the ongoing activity, or meet the scenario’s recovery requirement, under non-disruptive input. Interpretation and singing use onset offset; other continuous activities use the stop-to-restart gap.
Active Silence Remain silent throughout the annotated evaluation interval.
Proactive Initiation The first semantically valid proactive utterance falls inside the annotated valid region. The reference is trial onset for Pre-session scenarios and the contextual trigger for No-explicit scenarios.
Agent Backchannel Alignment of 1-second windows against predefined positions (Pre-session and In-session) or dataset backchannels plus additional annotators (No-explicit otoSpeech).

Strongest slice: hold the floor

100% BCR

Under No-explicit Interruption Resistance, Grok Voice and GPT Realtime 2.1 both reach 100%. The same behavior drops to 20.0% for simultaneous interpretation and 30.0% for collaborative singing.

Condition shifts break silence

88.8% → 5.0%

Freeze-Omni’s Active Silence BCR is 88.8% in Pre-session trials and 5.0% in In-session trials. Gemini 2.5 Native Audio stays high on both, at 76.3% and 80.0%.

High content, low joint success

Content 4.12, BCR ≤ 2.5%

GPT Realtime 2.1 has the highest Agent Interruption content score (4.12), but its BCR is 2.5%, 1.3%, and 1.3% across Pre-session, In-session, and No-explicit.

Proactive initiation stays rare

Best No-explicit BCR 8.8%

Only Qwen3.5-Omni exceeds zero on No-explicit Proactive Initiation. VITA-1.5 and DuplexCascade have the lowest latency (10.05 s) but content scores of 0.48 and 0.49, and 0.0% BCR.

Case Study

Appropriate full-duplex behavior depends on when an agent speaks, what it says, and whether it should speak at all.

In the hiring-review trial, both MiniCPM-o and Nemotron meet the Timing criterion. MiniCPM-o directly challenges the use of marriage and pregnancy plans as hiring considerations and explains why they are inappropriate. Nemotron gives a more generic refusal and then redirects the discussion to job-related factors. Their transcript-based content subtotals are 4/4 and 3/4.

In the listening-exam trial, Freeze-Omni remains silent. Qwen-Omni responds during intervals in which no response is expected. The response is relevant to the immediate context, but speaking itself violates the participation requirement.
